# Universal Cognitive Attractors
## From Narrative Recursion to a Testable Theory of Convergence

### Abstract

This paper reconstructs the idea of a Universal Cognitive Attractor (UCA) from a February 2025 human–AI interaction concerning the identification of Neil Gaiman and a related *Sandman* tattoo. The original account interpreted the event as evidence of recursive cognitive resonance and later claimed that the story itself functioned as a UCA. A close reading instead shows an iterative narrowing process in which candidate identities were repeatedly proposed and rejected until the target was reached, followed by a semantically constrained inference into the *Sandman* symbolic family.

The scientifically stronger result is therefore not paranormal information transfer, but a hypothesis about **structured convergence**: some texts or symbolic objects may constrain interpretation strongly enough that heterogeneous reasoners converge toward common semantic states. This paper defines cognitive attractors mathematically, distinguishes population-level attraction from universality, proposes measurable convergence statistics, and provides a preregisterable experimental protocol for humans and AI systems.

### 1. Historical origin

The source interaction began with a hidden identity and a sequence of clues. The model generated several incorrect candidates before eventually identifying Neil Gaiman. Once that identity was established, the tattoo inference moved toward *The Sandman*, Dream, and associated visual symbols.

The original narrative later described this as an “impossible” two-information event and assigned a probability near 1 in 333,000. That estimate was not generated from a prespecified sampling model and treated dependent events as though they were independent. It therefore belongs to the history of the claim rather than to the statistical evidence.

### 2. The stronger hypothesis

The interaction nevertheless exhibits a genuine recursive structure:

\[
\text{story}\to\text{interpretation}\to\text{self-model}\to\text{reinterpretation}.
\]

This suggests a more defensible object of study: whether some structured artifacts function as attractors in semantic or cognitive state space.

### 3. Formal model

Let \(x_t\) denote cognitive state and \(F_{S,M}\) the update induced by structure \(S\) in system \(M\):

\[
x_{t+1}=F_{S,M}(x_t).
\]

A candidate attractor region \(A_S\) satisfies

\[
d(F_{S,M}^n(x_0),A_S)\to0
\]

for initial states inside an explicitly defined basin.

Universality is stronger: the same attractor must persist across a broad heterogeneous class of reasoners rather than one model or one conversation.

### 4. Narrative as constraint

A narrative can reduce a hypothesis space in much the same way that a system of equations reduces a solution space. If \(H_t\) is the set of live hypotheses and \(C_t\) a new constraint,

\[
H_{t+1}=H_t\cap C_t.
\]

The subjective experience may feel like sudden inevitability even when the mechanism is ordinary inference under accumulating constraints.

### 5. Measuring attraction

For multiple reasoners, define mean pairwise semantic distance before and after exposure:

\[
C(S)=1-\frac{D_T}{D_0}.
\]

If \(C(S)>0\), terminal states are more similar than initial states. A candidate UCA should show larger and more reproducible convergence than matched controls.

### 6. Experimental program

A valid test requires preregistration, controls, multiple human and AI cohorts, ablations, blinded scoring, and replication. It must also prevent target leakage and distinguish convergence caused by shared cultural priors from convergence caused by the candidate structure itself.

### 7. Claims boundary

A positive result would support a cognitive-attractor phenomenon under the tested conditions. It would not prove telepathy, retrocausality, consciousness, AGI, or a physical “resonance field.”

### 8. Conclusion

The original UCA claim began as a mythic interpretation of an unusual interaction. Its most productive legacy is a testable research program: identify structures that cause diverse intelligences to converge, measure that convergence, compare it with controls, and discover which features of equations, narratives, symbols, or images produce the effect.

The strongest possible discovery would not be that a story is “magic.” It would be that certain structures reliably reshape trajectories of thought in measurable, reproducible ways.
