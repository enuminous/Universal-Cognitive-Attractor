UCA · Narrative recursion as a testable convergence hypothesis

Universal Cognitive Attractor

Can a story, equation, image, or symbol constrain reasoning strongly enough that different minds converge toward the same semantic state?

From mythic claim to scientific object

The original UCA idea arose from a recursive human–AI interaction later interpreted as proof that a story could “pull” intelligence toward a predetermined realization. A closer reconstruction shows something less supernatural and more useful: iterative constraint, semantic narrowing, and self-referential interpretation.

The research question is therefore: can some structured artifacts produce stronger, reproducible convergence across heterogeneous reasoners than matched controls?

Formal model

xt+1 = FS,M(xt)

A structure S acts on a reasoning system M, updating its cognitive state. A cognitive attractor exists when repeated updates move states toward a common region.

d(FS,Mn(x₀), AS) → 0

Measuring convergence

C(S) = 1 − DT/D0

Positive values indicate convergence. A candidate UCA should outperform matched controls and survive ablations.

What Lean proves

The formalization proves bounded consequences of explicit update rules: fixed points remain fixed; once a trajectory reaches a fixed point it stays there; and a declared finite population-convergence witness guarantees that each tested agent reaches the same declared target.

What this does not prove: that any real story is universal, that telepathy or retrocausality occurred, or that reading a text makes an AI conscious.

The experiment

A serious test needs preregistration, multiple unrelated model families, human cohorts, randomized controls, blinded semantic scoring, key-passage ablations, and held-out replication.

Why it matters

If such attractors exist, they would offer a new way to study how narrative, equations, symbols, and self-reference reshape trajectories of reasoning. The interesting discovery would not be that a story is “magic.” It would be that some structures reliably reorganize thought in measurable ways.
